# Income Tax Benefits on Loans in India (Public Information)

> Public information: a plain-language summary of income-tax deductions on loans. Section numbers refer to the Income-tax Act, 1961; the Income-tax Act, 2025, which applies from 1 April 2026, renumbers these provisions. General information, not tax advice — consult a tax professional for your situation. Last reviewed: October 2026.

## Old vs New Tax Regime

Most loan-related deductions are available only under the old tax regime. The new tax regime, which is the default, has lower tax rates but does not allow the principal deduction (Section 80C), the self-occupied home loan interest deduction, or the education loan interest deduction (Section 80E). A taxpayer can compare both and choose.

## Home Loan

Under the old regime: principal repaid on a home loan can be claimed under Section 80C, within the overall ₹1.5 lakh limit (shared with PPF, ELSS, life insurance, etc.); interest paid on a loan for a self-occupied house can be deducted up to ₹2 lakh a year under Section 24(b). For a let-out property, interest is deductible against rental income under both regimes, but the loss that can be set off against other income is limited. Joint home-loan borrowers who are also co-owners can each claim these deductions separately.

## Education Loan

Under the old regime, the entire interest paid on an education loan from a bank or approved institution — for higher studies of yourself, your spouse, your children, or a student you are the legal guardian of — is deductible under Section 80E, with no upper limit. The deduction is available for up to 8 years, starting from the year repayment begins. The principal repaid is not deductible.

## Personal, Car and Gold Loans

There is generally no tax deduction for personal, car or gold loans used for personal purposes. If the money is used for business, or to buy, build or renovate a house, the interest may be deductible under the relevant business or house-property rules, with supporting proof.

## Interest Certificate

To claim home or education loan deductions, ask the bank for an interest certificate (a provisional certificate during the year and a final certificate after 31 March) showing the principal and interest paid.
