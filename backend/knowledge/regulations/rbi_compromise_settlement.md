# Loan Settlement (One-Time Settlement) vs Loan Closure (Public Information)

> Public information: a plain-language summary of the RBI circular "Framework for Compromise Settlements and Technical Write-offs" (RBI/2023-24/40, 8 June 2023) and of how settlements are reported to credit bureaus. Applies to banks in India. General information, not legal or financial advice — check rbi.org.in for the current text. Last reviewed: October 2026.

## What a Settlement Is

A compromise settlement, often called a one-time settlement (OTS), is an agreement where a borrower who cannot repay in full pays a negotiated amount, and the lender gives up the rest of its claim. It is different from closing a loan, where the borrower repays everything owed. A settlement is usually offered only on loans that are already seriously overdue or classified as NPA.

## RBI Rules for Settlements

Every bank must have a Board-approved policy that sets which loans can be settled, how much the bank may give up, and who can approve it — always someone at least one level above the official who sanctioned the loan. A settlement whose payments are spread over more than three months is treated as a restructuring. Loans classified as fraud or wilful default can also be settled, but that does not stop any criminal proceedings.

## Cooling Period Before New Credit

After a settlement, a borrower must wait for a cooling period before the same lender can give them fresh credit. For loans other than farm credit, this cooling period is at least 12 months, and banks may set a longer period in their policy.

## Effect on Your Credit Score

A settled loan is reported to the credit bureaus as "settled", not "closed". A "settled" status tells future lenders that the full amount was not repaid, so it lowers the credit score and makes new loans harder to get. A loan repaid in full is reported as "closed", which is not held against the borrower.

## Practical Advice

Before agreeing to a settlement, get the offer in writing, including the exact amount, the payment dates and confirmation that no further dues will be claimed. After the final payment, collect a settlement or no-dues letter. A borrower who can manage the full dues — even over a longer period through restructuring — will usually protect their credit history better than through a settlement.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I settle my overdue loan for a lower amount?**
Banks may offer a settlement on seriously overdue loans under their Board-approved policy, but it is the bank's decision. A settlement is reported as "settled" and lowers your credit score, so ask the bank about restructuring or a revised repayment plan first.

**What is the difference between "settled" and "closed" on my credit report?**
"Closed" means you repaid the loan in full. "Settled" means the lender accepted less than the full amount. Lenders view "settled" negatively when you apply for new credit.
