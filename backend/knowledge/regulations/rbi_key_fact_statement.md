# Key Fact Statement (KFS) and Annual Percentage Rate (Public Information)

> Public information: a plain-language summary of the RBI circular on Key Fact Statement for loans and advances (15 April 2024). Applies to all banks and regulated lenders in India. General information, not legal advice — check rbi.org.in for the current text. Last reviewed: October 2026.

## What Is a Key Fact Statement

A Key Fact Statement (KFS) is a short, standard-format summary of a loan's key terms that the lender must give the borrower before the loan agreement is signed. Since 1 October 2024, a KFS is mandatory for all retail and MSME term loans, including home, personal, vehicle and education loans. It must be in a language the borrower understands.

## What the KFS Shows

The KFS lists the loan amount, tenure, interest rate and type (fixed or floating), the EMI and repayment schedule, all fees and charges (processing fee, insurance, legal or valuation charges), penal charges, prepayment or foreclosure charges, the cooling-off period for digital loans, and the lender's grievance redressal contact.

## Annual Percentage Rate (APR)

The KFS must show the Annual Percentage Rate (APR) — the all-in annual cost of the loan, including the interest rate and all fees and charges paid to the lender. The APR is the best single number for comparing loans from different lenders, because a loan with a lower interest rate but high fees can cost more overall.

## Charges Not in the KFS

Any fee or charge that is not mentioned in the KFS cannot be charged to the borrower at any point during the loan's life without the borrower's explicit consent.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long do I get to read the KFS?**
For loans with a tenure of seven days or more, the KFS must stay valid for at least three working days, so the borrower can compare and decide without pressure.

**Is the APR the same as the interest rate?**
No. The interest rate is only the cost of borrowing the money. The APR also includes fees and charges, so it is usually higher than the interest rate.
