# LoanMate Bank (Demo) — Business Loan

> This is a fictional demo product of "LoanMate Bank," created for the LoanMate AI academic project. It does not describe a real bank's policy. Figures are in line with typical ranges at Indian banks; government scheme details are public information.

## Overview

The LoanMate Business Loan funds working capital, equipment purchase, inventory or expansion for self-employed professionals, proprietors, partnerships and micro, small and medium enterprises (MSMEs).

## Interest Rate, Amount and Tenure

Unsecured business loans carry an interest rate of 11% to 18% per annum, for amounts from ₹1 lakh to ₹50 lakh and a tenure of 1 to 5 years. Secured business loans and loans against property are available at lower rates for larger amounts.

## Charges

A processing fee of up to 2% of the loan amount plus GST. Under RBI's 2025 prepayment directions, floating-rate business loans taken by individuals and micro and small enterprises from commercial banks carry no prepayment charges; fixed-rate business loans may carry a prepayment charge of 2% to 4% of the outstanding principal, as disclosed in the Key Fact Statement.

## Eligibility and Documents

Business running for at least 2 years, with a minimum annual turnover of ₹10 lakh and a credit score of 700 or above preferred. Documents: KYC of the business and owners, business registration (GST certificate, Udyam registration or trade licence), the last 2 years' IT returns with audited financials, and the last 12 months' bank statements.

## Government-Backed Options

Micro and small enterprises can get collateral-free loans under the Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises (CGTMSE), where the government trust guarantees a share of the loan. Small businesses can also apply for Pradhan Mantri Mudra Yojana (PMMY) loans for non-farm income-generating activities through banks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What happens if my business loan becomes overdue?**
The same RBI rules apply as for other loans: overdue accounts are reported as SMA and, after 90 days overdue, classified as NPA. Talk to the bank early about rescheduling if cash flow is tight.
