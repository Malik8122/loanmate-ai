# Ways to Pay an EMI: NACH, UPI AutoPay and E-Mandates (Public Information)

> Public information: a plain-language summary of how loan EMIs are collected in India, including the RBI's framework for processing e-mandates on recurring transactions (consolidated in April 2026) and Section 25 of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007. General information, not legal advice — check rbi.org.in and your loan agreement. Last reviewed: October 2026.

## Auto-Debit Through NACH

Most EMIs are collected through NACH (National Automated Clearing House), run by NPCI. The borrower signs a mandate allowing the lender to debit the EMI from their bank account on the due date every month. An e-NACH mandate can be signed online using net banking or a debit card. The only thing the borrower must do each month is keep enough balance in that account before the due date.

## UPI AutoPay and Card E-Mandates

EMIs can also be paid through a recurring mandate on UPI (UPI AutoPay) or on a card. Under RBI's e-mandate rules, registering a mandate needs additional authentication, such as a UPI PIN or OTP. Recurring payments up to ₹15,000 each can then go through without fresh authentication; loan EMIs above ₹15,000 need the customer to authenticate each debit. The customer is notified after every debit and can cancel or change the mandate from their UPI app or bank.

## Other Ways to Pay

An EMI can also be paid through the lender's own app or website, net banking, a branch, or a standing instruction on the customer's account. Pay only through the lender's official channels — never through a payment link sent by an unknown caller or message.

## When an Auto-Debit Fails

If there is not enough balance on the debit date, the mandate "bounces". The lender usually charges a bounce charge, the EMI becomes overdue, and penal charges may apply until it is paid. Under Section 25 of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007, dishonour of an electronic debit for insufficient funds can be treated like a cheque bounce. Repeated bounces are reported to credit bureaus and lower the credit score.

## Due Date on a Holiday

Auto-debits are processed on banking days. If the EMI date falls on a bank holiday, the debit is usually presented on the next working day — but the exact rule is set in the loan agreement, so keep the balance ready before the due date.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**My EMI is due on the 5th. When should I add money to my account?**
At least a day before the due date, so the auto-debit finds enough balance. A failed debit attracts a bounce charge and penal charges.

**Can I pay my EMI by UPI?**
Yes, through UPI AutoPay or a one-time UPI payment in the lender's official app. Recurring UPI debits above ₹15,000 need you to approve each payment.
