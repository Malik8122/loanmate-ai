# Floating Interest Rates, Repo-Linked Loans and EMI Reset (Public Information)

> Public information: a plain-language summary of RBI rules on external benchmark lending (circular of 4 September 2019) and the RBI circular "Reset of Floating Interest Rate on EMI based Personal Loans" (18 August 2023). Applies to all banks in India. General information, not legal advice. Last reviewed: October 2026.

## External Benchmark Linked Rate (EBLR)

Since 1 October 2019, every new floating-rate retail loan (home, personal, vehicle, education) and MSME loan from a bank must be linked to an external benchmark — usually the RBI's repo rate, or a government Treasury Bill yield published by FBIL. The bank adds a spread on top of the benchmark. The rate must be reset at least once every three months, so when the RBI changes the repo rate, the borrower's rate follows at the next reset.

## Older Benchmarks: MCLR and Base Rate

Loans taken earlier may still be linked to the bank's Marginal Cost of Funds based Lending Rate (MCLR, introduced April 2016) or the older Base Rate. These internal benchmarks pass on RBI rate changes more slowly. Borrowers on MCLR or Base Rate can ask their bank to switch to an EBLR-linked rate, usually for a nominal fee.

## When a Floating Rate Changes — Borrower Options

Under the RBI's 2023 rules, when a floating rate is reset the bank must clearly inform the borrower and offer a choice: increase the EMI, extend the tenure, or a mix of both; prepay part or all of the loan at any time; or switch to a fixed rate as per the bank's policy (any switching charge must be disclosed). The tenure cannot be extended so far that the EMI no longer covers the interest (negative amortisation).

## Quarterly Loan Statement

Banks must give borrowers of EMI-based floating-rate loans a statement at least every quarter showing the principal and interest paid so far, the EMI amount, the number of EMIs left, and the annualised interest rate for the full tenure.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**The RBI cut the repo rate. When will my EMI fall?**
For a repo-linked loan, at the next reset date, which comes at least once every three months. Many banks reduce the tenure rather than the EMI by default — you can ask the bank to reduce the EMI instead.

**Can I switch from a floating rate to a fixed rate?**
Yes, banks must offer the option at reset as per their board-approved policy. Fixed rates are usually higher, and a switching fee may apply; it must be disclosed upfront.
