# LoanMate Bank (Demo) — Gold Loan

> This is a fictional demo product of "LoanMate Bank," created for the LoanMate AI academic project. The interest rate and charges are demo values; the loan-to-value and gold-return rules summarise the public RBI (Lending Against Gold and Silver Collateral) Directions, 2025, which apply from 1 April 2026.

## Overview

The LoanMate Gold Loan is a secured loan against gold jewellery or coins. It is quick to sanction, needs little documentation, and the gold is kept in the bank's secure custody until the loan is repaid.

## Interest Rate and Tenure

In this demo policy, gold loans carry an interest rate of 8.5% to 14% per annum, for a tenure of up to 12 months. Customers can pay a regular EMI, pay interest monthly and the principal at the end, or repay everything in one bullet payment at the end of the tenure.

## Loan-to-Value (LTV) — RBI Limits

Under the RBI's 2025 gold-loan directions, for consumption loans the bank can lend up to 85% of the gold's value for loans up to ₹2.5 lakh, up to 80% for loans above ₹2.5 lakh and up to ₹5 lakh, and up to 75% for larger loans. For bullet-repayment loans, the interest due over the tenure is counted within this limit. Gold is valued at the average closing price of 22-carat gold of the previous 30 days or the previous day, whichever is lower; stones and other metals are not counted.

## Documents

KYC documents only. For small loans, if the customer does not have the original purchase receipt, a self-declaration of ownership is accepted. Gold bars and gold ETFs are not accepted as collateral.

## Return of Gold

After the loan is fully repaid, the gold must be returned within 7 working days. Under the RBI directions, if the delay is the bank's fault, the bank pays ₹5,000 per day of delay. If the loan is not repaid, the bank may auction the gold only after giving the borrower prior notice, and must return any surplus after recovering its dues.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much can I get on 20 grams of gold?**
It depends on the gold's purity and current price. For a loan up to ₹2.5 lakh, the bank can lend up to 85% of the gold's assessed value.
