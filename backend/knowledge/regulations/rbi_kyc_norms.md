# KYC (Know Your Customer) Rules for Bank Customers (Public Information)

> Public information: a plain-language summary of the RBI Master Direction – Know Your Customer (KYC) Direction, 2016, as amended. Applies to all banks in India. General information, not legal advice. Last reviewed: October 2026.

## Why Banks Need KYC

RBI rules require every bank to verify the identity and address of its customers and to keep that information up to date. If KYC is not updated when due, the bank may restrict some operations on the account until it is completed.

## Officially Valid Documents (OVDs)

Any one of these can be used as proof of identity and address: passport, driving licence, proof of possession of Aadhaar number, Voter ID card, NREGA job card signed by a State Government officer, or a letter from the National Population Register. A PAN card (or Form 60 if the customer has no PAN) is also required. If the address on the document is not current, other documents such as a recent utility bill can be used as deemed proof of address for a limited time.

## Periodic KYC Update

Banks must periodically update KYC based on the customer's risk category: at least once every 2 years for high-risk, every 8 years for medium-risk, and every 10 years for low-risk customers. If nothing has changed, a customer can usually update KYC with a simple self-declaration through email, registered mobile, net banking, ATM, or a letter — without visiting the branch.

## Ways to Complete KYC

KYC can be completed at any branch, through Aadhaar-based e-KYC, or through Video-based Customer Identification Process (V-CIP, or video KYC), where a bank official verifies the customer over a live video call through the bank's official app or website. Central KYC (CKYC) gives each customer a KYC Identifier Number (KIN) that can be reused across banks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Someone called saying my KYC will expire and asked for my OTP. Is that the bank?**
No. Banks never ask for an OTP, PIN, password or full card details over a call, SMS or link. That is a KYC fraud — do not share anything, and report it to the cyber fraud helpline 1930.

**Is Aadhaar compulsory for KYC?**
No. Aadhaar is one of several officially valid documents; a customer can use a passport, driving licence, Voter ID or the other listed documents instead.
