# RBI Rules on Penal Charges for Late Payment (Public Information)

> Public information: a plain-language summary of the RBI circular "Fair Lending Practice – Penal Charges in Loan Accounts" (RBI/2023-24/53, 18 August 2023). Applies to all banks and NBFCs in India. General information, not legal advice — check rbi.org.in for the current text. Last reviewed: October 2026.

## Penal Charges, Not Penal Interest

When a borrower misses an EMI or breaks a material term of the loan, a lender may levy a penalty — but only as a fixed "penal charge". It cannot be added as extra "penal interest" on top of the loan's interest rate. This rule applies to fresh loans from 1 April 2024, and existing loans had to be moved to it by 30 June 2024.

## No Interest on Penal Charges

Penal charges cannot be capitalised. That means no interest is charged on the penal charge itself — it does not compound. Normal interest on the overdue EMI amount continues as per the loan agreement.

## Reasonable and Disclosed

There is no fixed upper limit, but penal charges must be reasonable and in proportion to the default, and the same for all borrowers in a loan or product category. Penal charges for individuals cannot be higher than those for non-individual borrowers for similar non-compliance. The amount and the reason must be disclosed in the loan agreement and Key Fact Statement, shown on the lender's website, and communicated to the borrower whenever a reminder for the breach is sent.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**My bank added 2% penal interest to my loan rate after a missed EMI. Is that allowed?**
Not for loans covered by the 2023 RBI circular. A late payment penalty must be a separate penal charge, not an increase in the interest rate. You can raise a complaint with the bank and, if it is not resolved, with the RBI Ombudsman.

**Do penal charges apply to credit cards?**
No. Credit cards, external commercial borrowings, trade credits and structured obligations are covered by their own product-specific rules, not this circular.
