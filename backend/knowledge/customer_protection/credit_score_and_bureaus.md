# Credit Scores and Credit Bureaus in India (Public Information)

> Public information: a plain-language summary of how credit information companies work in India under the Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act, 2005 and RBI directions, including the RBI compensation framework for credit information complaints (circular of 26 October 2023). General information, not financial advice. Last reviewed: October 2026.

## The Four Credit Bureaus

India has four RBI-licensed credit information companies (CICs), also called credit bureaus: TransUnion CIBIL, Experian, Equifax and CRIF High Mark. Banks report every borrower's loan and credit card repayment history to them, and check them before approving a new loan.

## Credit Score

A credit score is a three-digit number, usually between 300 and 900, that summarises how reliably a person has repaid credit. Many lenders prefer a score of around 700–750 or above. The main factors are paying EMIs and card bills on time, how much of the available credit limit is used, the length of credit history, the mix of secured and unsecured loans, and how many new loans or cards were applied for recently.

## Free Credit Report

Every person is entitled to one free full credit report, including the credit score, once a year from each of the four credit bureaus. It can be requested on each bureau's official website. Checking your own report does not lower your score.

## Fixing Errors

If a credit report has a mistake, such as a loan shown as unpaid after it was closed, raise a dispute with the credit bureau or the lender. The lender has 21 days and the bureau 9 days — 30 days in total — to resolve it. If it is not resolved within 30 days, the customer is entitled to compensation of ₹100 for each day of delay.

## Alerts and Default Reporting

Banks must inform a borrower before reporting a default to the credit bureaus, and credit bureaus send SMS or email alerts when a customer's credit report is accessed by a lender.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will one late EMI affect my credit score?**
Yes, any EMI reported as overdue (even in the SMA-0 stage of up to 30 days) can lower the score. Paying as soon as possible and keeping later payments on time helps it recover.

**Does closing a loan early hurt my credit score?**
No. A loan closed in full is reported as closed and generally does not hurt the score.
