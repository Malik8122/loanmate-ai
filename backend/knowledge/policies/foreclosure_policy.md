# LoanMate Bank (Demo) — Foreclosure Policy

## What Is Foreclosure

Foreclosure means paying off your entire remaining loan balance in one lump sum, closing the loan completely before its original scheduled end date. This is different from a partial prepayment, which only reduces the balance without closing the loan.

## Eligibility for Foreclosure

Floating-rate loans taken by individuals can be foreclosed at any time, with no lock-in period, as required by RBI rules. For fixed-rate loans, foreclosure is allowed after a minimum lock-in period, which varies by loan type:

- Personal Loan: after 12 EMIs
- Home Loan: after 6 EMIs
- Vehicle Loan: after 12 EMIs
- Education Loan: after the moratorium period ends and at least 6 EMIs have been paid
- Business Loan: after 12 EMIs

## Foreclosure Charges

Floating-rate loans taken by individuals carry no foreclosure charge — RBI rules prohibit it. For fixed-rate loans, a foreclosure charge of 2% to 5% of the outstanding principal may apply, at the rate stated in the loan's Key Fact Statement.

## Section: Process

To foreclose a loan, a customer should:

1. Request a foreclosure quote, which shows the exact payoff amount as of a chosen date.
2. Make the full payment by the specified date.
3. Receive a no-dues certificate and loan closure confirmation from LoanMate.
4. Collect the original property documents. Under RBI rules they must be returned, and any charge on the property removed, within 30 days of closure — or the bank pays ₹5,000 per day of delay.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between prepayment and foreclosure?**
Prepayment is a partial extra payment that reduces your balance but keeps the loan open. Foreclosure is paying the entire remaining balance at once and closing the loan.

**Will foreclosure affect my credit score?**
Successfully foreclosing a loan is generally reported as "closed" and does not negatively affect your credit score; in fact, a clean closure history is typically viewed favourably.
